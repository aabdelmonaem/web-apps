Game Rules

Rules, scoring, and controls implemented in the Killer Sudoku web version.

1. Rows, columns, and boxes

Each row, column, and 3 × 3 box must contain every digit from 1 to 9 exactly once. Fixed starting clues cannot be edited. You win when all 81 cells match the puzzle’s solution.

2. Cage sums and unique digits

Every cell belongs to one connected dashed cage. Its corner number is the target sum. Digits in that cage must add up to the total and must not repeat, even across different rows, columns, or boxes. Cages contain up to five cells; some may contain only one.

3. Cage example

A two-cell cage totaling 10 could use 1 + 9, 2 + 8, 3 + 7, or 4 + 6. It cannot use 5 + 5. The cells’ rows, columns, and boxes determine which pair and order work.

4. Conflict colors and lives

Red entries indicate a Sudoku duplicate, a repeated cage digit, a sum over the cage total, or a full cage with the wrong sum. A partially filled cage that already reaches its total is also conflicting because the remaining cells need positive digits. These colors are separate from answer checking: every changed final answer that differs from the solution costs one of three lives. Re-entering the same value has no effect. Erasing does not restore lives.

5. Notes and erase

Notes toggles pencil marks in empty editable cells. Notes do not affect score or lives. Any changed nonzero final entry, including an incorrect entry, removes matching notes from its row, column, and box. A revealed hint does the same. The game does not automatically remove candidates based only on cage membership or sum combinations. Erase clears the selected editable cell’s value and notes.

6. Hints

Each game starts with three hints at every difficulty. A hint immediately reveals the selected empty or incorrect editable cell, or the first such cell when the selection is fixed or already correct. A hint earns no points and costs no lives. Hints are disabled when exhausted, paused, or finished.

7. Scoring

Every cell pays 100 points the first time you manually enter its correct answer, at every difficulty. Re-entering an already rewarded cell earns nothing. Incorrect entries, time, and hints do not deduct points. A hinted cell can still earn its one manual-entry reward if you later erase and correctly enter it and it has not previously been rewarded.

Scoring example

Five first-time correct entries earn 500 points. Two mistakes leave one life, and one hint leaves two hints; the score stays 500. Erasing and correctly re-entering an already rewarded answer leaves the score at 500.

8. Difficulties and generation

Easy, Medium, Hard, Expert, Master, and Extreme are unlocked. New games use uniquely solvable Sudoku starter clues with connected cages added to the solution. The starting-clue targets are 50, 40, 32, 26, 22, and 17 respectively. Extra clues may remain to preserve uniqueness. Difficulty controls clue targets and does not certify a solving technique; cage count is not a difficulty target in this web version.

9. Timer and pause

The timer stops during Pause, while the difficulty picker is open, and after a win or game over. Pause hides the digits and cage labels and blocks entries and hints. Switching tabs does not automatically pause the game; use Pause when taking a break.

10. Restart and New game

Restart preserves the current clues, cages, and difficulty. It clears entries and notes, resets score and time, restores three lives and three hints, turns Notes off, and resumes play. Choosing a difficulty in New game replaces the puzzle and cages. Closing the picker leaves your progress unchanged.

11. Progress and completion

Progress is stored in memory only, with no saved-game recovery. Reloading or navigating away discards it. Completing all cells correctly wins; a third mistake ends the game. Both outcomes block further entries and hints until Restart or New game.
