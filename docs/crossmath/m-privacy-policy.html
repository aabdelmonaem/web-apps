CrossMath Privacy Policy

Last updated: 13 November 2026

1. Who is responsible

Ahmed Elbari, an individual developer based in Qatar, provides CrossMath. In this policy, “I”, “me”, and “the developer” mean Ahmed Elbari. For privacy questions or requests, email a.abdelmonaem@gmail.com, stating the game name. This policy covers the installed game and its optional nearby challenges. A website hosting this policy may have its own hosting-provider practices.

2. Overview

The current version is free, has no advertising or in-app purchases, and requires no account. Solo puzzles run on your device. The game does not include developer-operated analytics, advertising trackers, or an automatic crash-reporting service. It does not upload your solo progress or nickname to a developer-operated server. Nearby play exchanges information with other players' devices, and contacting support sends information through email. These are described below.

3. Information used on your device

The game processes and stores the following in its local application storage:

  • Profile and preferences: your nickname, appearance, sound, haptic and animation preferences, and other game settings.
  • Progress and records: arithmetic puzzles, number-tile placements, undo history, remaining hearts and hints, and puzzle completion records, including difficulty, timers, game identifiers, and statistics where supported.
  • Nearby play: a randomly generated peer identifier saved for reconnecting, challenge identifiers and lobby codes, the other player's displayed name and peer identifier, puzzle details, connection or readiness state, and your and your opponent's results where needed to resume a challenge.

These records let the game remember your choices, continue unfinished puzzles, calculate scores and records, and reconnect nearby challenges. They are not an online account. The nearby peer identifier is generated by the game; it is not an advertising identifier or your device's hardware serial number.

4. Device-name nickname suggestion

If no nickname has been saved, the game attempts to read the device name made available by the operating system and uses an available name as the initial nickname. It preserves a nickname you have already saved. If the name is unavailable or restricted, it uses a default player name. Operating systems, particularly iOS, may restrict access to a custom device name.

A device name may contain your real name. Review and change the nickname in the profile before using nearby play if you prefer a pseudonym. A suggested nickname used for nearby play can be visible to other nearby devices. Reading the device name does not give the game access to your contacts or an Apple or Google account profile.

5. Optional nearby challenges and recipients

When you use nearby discovery or hosting, your nickname, peer identifier, service and connection details may be advertised to or discovered by other compatible nearby devices, including before a challenge is accepted. Local communication can process network addresses, ports, Bluetooth identifiers, and signal information to find and connect devices.

A connected opponent receives the shared puzzle or information needed to reconstruct it, challenge identifiers, player names, readiness and start messages, and results. Results may include completion status, score, elapsed time, mistakes, hints used, and moves, depending on the game. Some of this information is saved on both devices for resuming or comparing results. There is no public online leaderboard operated by the developer.

Use nearby play with people you trust, keep lobby codes private, and avoid using sensitive information as a nickname. The developer cannot remotely remove information from another player's device or prevent that player from recording information already displayed. Leaving a challenge stops participation; any nickname or results already seen or retained by the other player cannot be recalled.

6. Permissions

Bluetooth or Nearby Devices permissions, local-network access, and Wi-Fi/network capabilities support optional nearby discovery and communication. Some older Android versions require location permission or location services to enable Bluetooth discovery. The game does not use this permission to obtain GPS coordinates or build a location history.

You may refuse or revoke these permissions in device settings. Nearby features may then be unavailable, while solo play remains available. The presence of an internet permission also supports local-network sockets; it does not mean that solo progress is uploaded to the developer. Device-name access remains subject to operating-system restrictions.

7. Support email and other providers

If you choose Contact, the game opens your email application; it does not send a message automatically. If you send an email, the developer receives your email address, message, and any name, screenshots, logs, or other information you include. This information is used to respond, investigate problems, handle privacy requests, and meet applicable legal obligations. Do not send passwords, private lobby codes, or unnecessary personal information.

The support mailbox uses Gmail. Google and your own email provider process messages under their applicable terms and privacy practices. Google's Privacy Policy explains Google's processing, including international processing. Support messages may be processed outside Qatar, including in the United States and other countries where those providers operate.

Apple, Google, your operating-system provider, and your backup or email provider may separately process download, diagnostic, backup, or service information according to their settings and policies. The game does not add a separate advertising or analytics SDK. The developer does not sell personal information, use it for targeted advertising, or send marketing emails. Support correspondence may be disclosed when reasonably necessary to comply with applicable law or a valid legal process; such disclosure is limited to what is required.

8. Retention and deletion

Local progress and preferences remain until overwritten, reset by an available game control, or removed using device storage controls. Nearby challenge state may remain so that a challenge can resume; use Leave or the equivalent challenge control to end the saved session where available. A game reset is not a complete deletion of all settings, statistics, or nearby identifiers.

To remove all local application records, use Android's Clear storage/Clear app data control, or delete the app and its data on iOS. Offloading an iOS app can retain data. Operating-system backups may retain or restore a copy; manage or delete those backups separately using your provider's controls. The developer does not operate a cloud backup or have remote access to your local saves.

Support correspondence is retained only as long as reasonably needed to respond to and document the request, resolve disputes, and meet legal obligations. You may request deletion by email. Information legally required to be retained may be kept for the necessary period; the developer will explain any applicable restriction. Email-provider backups and the other player's copies are subject to their own retention practices.

9. Your choices and privacy rights

You can change your nickname, stop nearby discovery or leave a challenge, revoke permissions in device settings, and delete local data as described above. For information held by the developer, you can request access, correction, deletion, or other rights available under applicable law. Where processing relies on consent, you may withdraw it; withdrawal does not undo earlier lawful processing. Only information reasonably needed to verify and handle a request will be requested. No account or extra personal identification is needed simply to play solo.

If you have a concern, contact the developer first if you wish. You may also complain to the relevant privacy authority. In Qatar, information is available from the National Cyber Security Agency's Personal Data Privacy Protection Department. Rights depend on applicable law, and local records accessible only on your device must generally be managed there.

10. General audience and children

The game is a general-audience puzzle game, not a service specifically directed to children. It does not ask players for their age or require a real name. Parents and guardians should supervise nearby play and support communications, and help children choose a non-identifying nickname. If you believe a child has sent personal information to the developer without necessary parental permission, contact the support address so the issue can be investigated and the information deleted where required. Device-local processing and nearby sharing still apply to younger players; this policy does not claim that no child-related information is ever processed.

11. Security and policy changes

The game uses application storage and its nearby communication mechanisms to support play. No device, network, or storage method is guaranteed secure. Protect your device and backups, and avoid sharing sensitive information through player names or support attachments.

This policy may be updated when the game's behavior or legal requirements change. The updated policy will show a revised date and will be made available with the game or through its policy link. Where required, additional notice or consent will be provided before materially different processing begins. Future advertising, purchases, or additional data services would require a fresh review and appropriate disclosures before introduction.
